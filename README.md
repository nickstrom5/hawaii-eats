# hawaii-eats

Three things built from one pipeline (modeled on `../id-eats/`, `../wi-eats/` and `../co-eats/`, which are never modified):

1. **The web leaderboard**: every Hawaii restaurant ranked on several boards, published as a private claude.ai Artifact from `site/`
   (generated by `scripts/make-leaderboard.py`). It uses the UCSD Google Local 2021 snapshot for ratings, labeled "2021" everywhere, so
   `site/` never goes in a public repo.
2. **Hawaiʻi Eats: Restaurants**, a free iPhone and iPad app (`HawaiiEats/`, XcodeGen `project.yml`). No Google-derived data: places come
   from Overture Maps open data, island coastlines and town spellings from the Hawaii Statewide GIS Program and USGS; ratings and hours come
   live from Apple Maps' own place card.
3. **The website** in `docs/` (generated by `scripts/make-site.py`): landing page, the hand-checked guides, the James Beard honorees, town
   and island pages, what Hawaii's green, yellow and red health placards mean, the web app at `/explore/`, privacy and terms. QA with
   `scripts/qa-site.py`.

See `CLAUDE.md` for the rules and commands, and `playbook/` for sources and calibration (`12-sources.md`) and the naming round
(`05-naming.md`).

## Official records
- **Hawaii State Department of Health, Food Safety Branch**: inspects every food business on every island and posts a color-coded placard
  (HAR 11-50). It publishes results one search at a time, so there are no inspection results here; each place links DOH's page.
- **DOH food permit lists** (2011 statewide, 2013 Oʻahu, opendata.hawaii.gov): history notes and calibration only. Permit holders' names
  and phone numbers are never shown.
- **Liquor licenses** are county-run and no list is readable automatically, so "serves alcohol" is unknown for now.
- **James Beard Foundation** honors and America's Classics, as facts. Hawaii has no MICHELIN Guide.

## Hand-checked research
`data/research/` (private): plate lunch and drive-ins, poke, saimin, shave ice, malasadas and bakeries, Hawaiian food and okazuya, musubi,
loco moco, Lahaina places open again after the 2023 fire, and the oldest places, each with a 2025–26 source.
